# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file. It mirrors the structure of the in-app [Change Log](https://satisfactory-factories.app/changelog) — same sections, full technical detail. For the release history prior to Beta v0.5 (the 0.1.x–0.2.x scaffolding releases), see the [GitHub commit history](https://github.com/satisfactory-factories/application/commits/main).

## Beta v0.7: Realtime sync, rooms and offline mode

Cloud saving is gone and live plans have taken its place. A tab you choose to sync lives on the server, follows your account to every device, and can be handed to a friend as a link you both edit at the same time. Offline is a proper mode rather than a failure state, your settings follow your account, and the backend has been rewritten from the ground up.

### Every tab is now local, synced or shared

- The old model saved one tab as a blob every ten seconds and the last writer won, so two devices could quietly overwrite each other and the only fix was a manual force download. That is replaced by **three kinds of tab, and you pick**. A **local** tab lives in this browser and needs no account, exactly like every tab did before. A **synced** tab lives on your account and appears on every device you sign in on. A **shared** tab is a synced tab you have invited other people into, and everyone edits the same plan live.
- The **+ button now opens a chooser** rather than silently making a local tab. It explains what each kind is, and a one-time dot on the button points it out the first time. Local stays the default, so nothing changes for anyone who never signs in.
- **Each tab wears its kind in the tab bar**: a monitor for local, a single person for synced, a group of people for shared. A shared tab also shows how many people are in it right now.
- **A local tab keeps its identity when you sync it.** Its ID becomes the plan's ID on the server, so nothing about it is recreated or renamed.
- Synced tabs still write their full contents to this browser exactly as before. That copy is what draws the screen, and it is what you would be left holding if the server were unreachable.

### Sharing: two links that are never mixed up

- The share dialog now offers two clearly separated things. **Copy snapshot link** is what `/share` has always been: a frozen copy of the plan as it is right now, which whoever opens it keeps as their own. It works on any tab and needs no account.
- **Invite collaborators** is new. A synced tab gets a link of the form `satisfactory-factories.app/room/three-word-slug`, and anyone who opens it is editing *your* plan with you. You can set your own words for the link, and the dialog tells you live whether they are free.
- **An invite can carry a password.** Set one and anyone new is asked for it once. Change it and anyone who joined anonymously is dropped while people signed in keep their access. Remove it and the link is open again.
- **Stop sharing puts the plan back to private.** Everyone else is removed and keeps their own copy of the last state they saw, and the tab in their bar quietly becomes a local one with a note saying why. Sharing again restores the same link.
- Deleting a shared plan does the same thing to everyone in it. Nobody ever loses data because of something the owner did.
- **Duplicate as a local tab** is available on any synced or shared tab, at any time, for an independent copy that answers to nobody.

### Editing together

- Changes flow both ways over a single connection and land on the other side in about as long as the network takes. You can both work in the same plan at once.
- **Edits to different factories both survive.** Edits to the same factory settle on one of them rather than merging into something neither of you asked for.
- What you see is always your last acknowledged state from the server, plus everything you have changed since, fully recalculated. A change of yours is never dropped in favour of an incoming one, and nothing is ever half-applied.
- The owner can rename, share, unshare, set the link, set a password and delete. Everyone else can edit the plan and leave. Renames reach every device.

### Offline mode

- **A switch in the account panel puts the planner into offline mode**, which means exactly no contact with the server: no connection, no requests, no retries. Keep planning; everything is kept on this device.
- If the connection drops on its own, a small bar asks whether you want to go offline rather than deciding for you. Say no and it keeps quietly retrying.
- **Coming back online is manual, like a phone.** When you switch it off, the planner reconnects, takes the server's current state, re-applies everything you changed while you were away, recalculates and sends it. Edits made offline survive closing the browser.
- Nothing about this blocks the planner. There are no popups to dismiss and no dialog in the way of your plan.

### Your settings follow your account

- Preferences that are about *you* rather than about this computer now travel with your account: satisfaction breakdowns, the group colours you have used, the tutorial and introduction you have already dismissed, the statistics panels you keep hidden and the summary you keep collapsed. Sign in on a new machine and the planner is set up the way you left it.
- Settings this browser has that your account has never seen are kept and uploaded rather than overwritten, so signing in for the first time on a machine never wipes what was already there.

### Account panel

- The account tile has been rebuilt: your username, a live connection indicator, the offline switch, the list of your synced plans with a share button on each, and **Change password**.
- **Recover server copy** brings back the single plan older versions of the planner saved to your account, on demand. If you sign in with an account that has one saved plan and this browser has nothing in it, it is brought back automatically.
- Signing in offers to sync any local plans the server does not know about, one at a time, and never forces it. Say no and they stay exactly as they are. Names that would collide get a "(local)" suffix rather than being merged.
- The out-of-sync dialog and the force download button are gone. Neither has anything to do now.

### Loading

- The staggered load only runs when something was actually calculated. Switching to a tab the server has already confirmed is current renders it immediately.
- Plan data arriving from the server goes through the same loading and validation path as a plan opened from this browser, instead of being written in behind it.

### Under the hood

- **The backend is a new application.** It has been rewritten in NestJS with a real module structure, typed configuration, graceful shutdown, and a test suite covering the routes, the live connection, concurrent edits, passwords, revocation, deletion and the hourly cleanup.
- A new shared package holds the message formats, the plan schema and the protocol version that the planner and the server both build against, so the two can no longer drift apart.
- Every plan that reaches the server is validated against one schema with explicit limits: 300 factories per plan, 10 plans of your own, 25 plans in your tab bar, and the same name and note truncation the planner has always applied.
- Multi-step changes are built as steps that can each be repeated safely, so a request that fails halfway leaves nothing stranded and simply resumes. Deleting a plan marks it dead first, which makes it inert instantly, and clears up afterwards.
- Who changed what, and when, is recorded per plan. There is no history view yet; the record is being kept from day one so that there can be.
- Every request now carries the app version and is refused if it is out of date, and an out-of-date tab shows a persistent "refresh to continue" bar instead of failing silently.
- `/save` and `/load` are retired. `/hello` is gone, since `/health` had already replaced it.

## [Unreleased]

### Factory icons

- Every factory drew the same generic industry glyph, so a plan of twenty factories showed twenty identical icons and the only thing distinguishing a sidebar row was a truncated name. Factories now carry an **icon you choose**, and it shows everywhere the factory is referenced: the card header beside the name, the sidebar row, the Factories Summary table, the graph node, the import rows, and the import/export chips on collapsed cards and in the satisfaction table.
- **352 icons to pick from.** Real game art for every machine, extractor and generator, every belt, lift, splitter, pipe and pump, all the power infrastructure, the stations and storage, the vehicles, the equipment, and every raw resource, fluid and component the planner knows about — plus emoji: coloured squares, circles, diamonds and triangles, the digits 0–10, and a row of symbols. No uploads: a shared plan can only ever carry an icon the app already ships, so there is nothing to process on anyone's behalf.
- **The picker opens on everything.** The first view is the whole set, laid out under category headings, so you can scroll it like a sheet rather than hunting through tabs for where something lives. A row of category buttons — All, Buildings, Power, Logistics, Vehicles, Raw Resources, Fluids, Components, Equipment, Emoji — narrows it when you want that, and stays on screen the whole time so the categories double as a map of what is in there.
- **Search covers everything at once, and filters as you type.** A query searches the whole set regardless of which category is selected, so a narrowed view never hides a match; picking a category clears the query again. Emoji carry keywords, so "red" finds the red square, the red circle and the red triangles, and "nuclear" finds the radiation symbol. Names are matched with their punctuation stripped too, because the game writes "Mk.5" and nobody types the dot — "conveyor belt mk 5" and "mk5" both find it.
- Click the icon in the card header or the sidebar to open the picker; it takes the same hover fill as the clickable item and building images elsewhere in the app. A tile applies immediately and closes, and "Use default" puts the generic glyph back.
- **A factory stores only an icon ID.** Everything about how that ID is drawn — the artwork it points at, its label, its grouping, even which emoji character it means — lives in one generated registry file. Plans in local storage, in the cloud and in share links can't be migrated, so nothing about rendering is baked into what gets saved: the artwork behind an icon can be changed later without touching a single plan.
- The registry is generated from the shipped artwork rather than from the game data, which means an item whose image was never shipped simply isn't offered. That caught the FICSMAS Gift, which is not flagged as a FICSMAS item and would have rendered as a broken image. A test asserts every icon in the registry resolves to a file that exists, so a bad entry fails the build instead of reaching the picker.
- Plans saved before this change are untouched and show the default glyph until an icon is picked.
- **The Demo plan wears them.** Every factory in it carries the icon of the thing it makes, and the copper chain sits in its own copper-coloured group, so both features are visible in the first plan anyone opens. The two nuclear factories dropped the ☢️ from their names with it — the nuclear plant and fuel rod icons say the same thing better.

### Factory groups

- A plan of thirty factories was a flat list. The only ways to organise it were the name and the drag order, and that order was one long sequence with nothing marking where one concern ended and the next began. Factories now belong to a **group**: a named, coloured folder that collapses like a toggle, reorders, and can be dragged in and out of. Anything in no group falls into **Ungrouped**, pinned to the top.
- **In the sidebar**, each group is a header with its colour swatch, an editable name, a factory count, a delete button, and a second line showing what the group makes — as many item icons as the sidebar is currently wide enough for, then a `+N` tile whose tooltip lists the rest. Widen or narrow the sidebar and the row re-fits as you drag, always filling exactly one line. Drag a factory between groups, reorder factories inside one, or drag the groups themselves. A group with no rows to aim at — collapsed, or simply empty — shows a dashed drop strip so it can still be dropped into.
- **The sidebar draws a group as a tree.** A line in the group's colour runs down the very edge of the section, starting at the header, with an elbow into each member factory and a corner at the last one — the shape a `tree` listing has, for the same reason: it says where a group starts and stops without a box around it. The rows themselves are unchanged, just indented off the line, and each elbow meets the middle of its row rather than its first line, so a factory carrying status chips is still pointed at squarely.
- **Collapsing a group is now instant.** It used to take seconds on a large group, because whether a group was open was stored on the group record — which is copied onto every member factory — so shutting a forty-factory group rewrote forty factory records, ran forty saves and forty metrics recalculations, and then tore down and rebuilt forty planner cards. Collapse is view state now: it lives on its own in local storage, touches nothing in the plan, and hides the cards rather than destroying them. A group that is already shut when a plan loads never renders its cards at all, so a plan kept mostly collapsed opens faster too. The trade-off is that collapse no longer travels with a shared plan or a cloud restore — it is per-browser, which is what it always should have been.
- **In the planner**, cards sit under a collapsible heading band per group, Ungrouped first. Bands only appear once there is more than one section, so a plan that has never used groups looks exactly as it did.
- **Colour.** Pick from a palette or any colour you like through the picker. Red and amber are deliberately not offered: they are the problem and warning tiers, and a group wearing one would read as a broken factory. A group's colour gives its cards a muted header and a bright left spine — **and a status still wins the border and the header**, so a factory short of copper stays red while its spine keeps saying which group it is in.
- **Assigning** is either a drag, or the new group chip on a factory's header, which lists the groups and can make a new one on the spot. It sits on the title line beside the factory icon, wearing the group's colour — deliberately not down among the status chips, because where a factory lives is not a thing that has gone wrong with it. A factory in no group says so: the chip reads *Ungrouped*.
- **Multi-group edit** does the same thing to a selection. Organising an existing plan one chip at a time meant opening forty cards in turn, so there is now a button beside **+ Group** that lists every factory grouped by where it currently sits, with *Select these* on each section for the case it is nearly always going to be — everything already in one group, or everything not in a group yet. Assigning does not close the dialog: the list re-sections in place and a toast says what moved, so several groups can be filled in one visit.
- **Deleting a group never deletes a factory.** An empty group goes immediately; one that still holds factories asks where they should go first — Ungrouped, or another group.
- Groups travel with the plan through save, share links, templates and cloud sync, with no migration and nothing to set up. Plans made before this change load untouched, entirely ungrouped.
- One known limitation: a group you have created but not yet put anything in has no factory to travel on, so it will not appear on a different machine until it has at least one member.

### Backend — a health check that can actually fail

- The API had one health route, `GET /hello`, and it returned `200` for as long as the Node process was alive. It never spoke to Mongo, so the database could be dead, unreachable or out of disk and the monitoring would still show green — which is exactly what happened when the API box filled its disk. New `GET /health` runs a real `ping` against Mongo (the `SELECT 1` equivalent) and returns **503** with the error message when it doesn't answer, so an outage raises an alert instead of nothing. The response also carries process uptime, the Mongoose connection state and how long the ping took.
- The ping is raced against a 3 second timeout. Mongoose is configured with `bufferCommands`, so with the database down a command doesn't fail — it queues for ten seconds, which is longer than any monitor or the Docker healthcheck will wait, and a dead database would have looked like a slow one.
- `/health` gets its own rate limit of 10 requests a minute and is exempt from the global limiter, so no amount of other traffic can push a monitor into a `429` and a false outage.
- The production container's Docker healthcheck now probes `/health` rather than `/hello`, so `docker ps` reports unhealthy when Mongo dies rather than only when the process does. Consequence: a deploy attempted while Mongo is down now fails at `up -d --wait` instead of going green. **The box's compose file is a hand-maintained mirror and no deploy syncs it** — see `docs/deployment.md`.
- `/hello` stays as-is, as a pure liveness route.

### Factory status indicators

- A factory in the sidebar used to tell you exactly one thing: it was red, or it wasn't. `hasProblem` was a single boolean, so three unrelated failures collapsed into one red blob and anything short of outright broken had nowhere to appear. Factories now carry a **status list**: named, severity-tiered conditions rendered as chips under the factory name, so a 30-factory plan can be triaged without opening a single card.
- **Two severity tiers.** Red for a problem — the factory can't do what the plan says it does, because some quantity doesn't add up. Amber for a warning — the plan is coherent, but something is probably not what you meant, or your world is behind your plan. The highest tier present paints the border; chips are not collapsed, so a factory that is both short of copper *and* out of sync shows both.
- **Six statuses to start with.** Red: part shortage, unmet export request, and building groups that don't add up. Amber: out of sync with the game, redundant import, and duplicate import. Game sync moved into the amber tier rather than being a state of its own — it shares the burnt orange it always wore, now a proper `--sf-status-warning` token instead of borrowing the building colour.
- **The chips say which items.** Every affected item shows its own icon alongside the count — three shortages are three icons and "3 shortages", not a generic warning glyph — up to four icons in the sidebar, with the full list in the chip's tooltip. The section headers inside the card show the same status in fuller form — up to six icons plus a longer label — so the sidebar can stay terse and the card can be specific. Clicking a chip scrolls to the section it points at.
- A factory with nothing wrong renders no chip row at all, rather than an empty one that still took its share of the spacing in the card header and left a gap before the first chip that *was* there.
- **The sidebar's minimum drag width went from 150px to 300px.** A chip carrying several icons and a label ("3 shortages") is a single unbreakable box, and the sidebar hides its horizontal overflow — below about 200px the label was silently cut off mid-word, which is the one thing the chip exists to say. A width saved below the new floor is raised to it on load rather than waiting for the next drag.
- **The sidebar entry grows and retracts** rather than snapping: the colour lands first, then the row expands to make room for the chip, and it collapses again when the condition clears. Loading a plan renders any existing chips already open, so a plan with a dozen broken factories doesn't play a dozen animations at once.
- The same treatment now applies everywhere factory state was already painted: the sidebar entry, the factory card's border and header, the section headers (Satisfaction, Imports, Products & Power), and the Factories Summary — which never painted the amber state at all, and whose factory names now carry a tooltip listing their statuses.
- **Fixed:** a factory whose *power generator* building groups didn't add up never turned red. The check has always run for power producers, but the rollup only ever looked at products, so a power-only factory could be visibly broken and still read as healthy.

### Sidebar — Active factory indicator

- The sidebar's factory list now shows which factory you're currently looking at: an orange bar (the same orange as the selected tab's underline) on the left edge of that factory's entry, following you as you scroll the plan or jump between factories — no more losing your place in a 30-factory plan. A factory counts as "in view" when its card spans an eye-line 10% down the planner pane (not the pane's very top edge), so a factory scrolled slightly past its header is still credited as the one being viewed rather than the tail end of the factory above it. The Statistics and Factories Summary jump-links get the same treatment when their sections are in view, and while the eye-line crosses the divider gap between two cards the indicator stays put on the last entry instead of blinking off. Works in both the desktop sidebar and the mobile navigation drawer.
- The thick divider between factory cards in the main view swapped its blue for the app header's burnt orange (now a shared `--sf-header` token, so the header, its gold border and the divider draw from one definition in `colors.ts`).
- In the Factories Summary sidebar row, the factory count chip and the fullscreen-summary expand control swapped places: the expand control now sits flush at the row's right edge, sized to the same 30px column as the factory rows' game-sync cells so the two line up down the sidebar.

### Export Calculator — Belts

- The Export Calculator gained a Belts transport method (returning from the pre-rework calculator, now per-destination): a full-width section beneath Train/Drone/Truck/Tractor showing how many conveyor belts of a chosen mark it takes to carry the export. All six marks (Mk.1–Mk.6 at 60/120/270/480/780/1,200 items per min) are selectable via an icon toggle, and the default mark auto-picks the smallest single belt that can carry the amount. Belt speeds are maintained in code — they aren't part of the game docs data. Fluid exports point to pipelines instead.
- Belt groups: an export can be split across multiple groups of belts, each with its own mark — group columns fill left to right (up to four per row, divided like the transport columns, each group's controls centred within its column). Each group offers two linked inputs — an items/min amount (with the shipped item's icon, ingredient-chip style) **OR** a whole number of belts to build (with the belt icon): entering an amount derives the belts to build (rounded up), entering a belt count sets the amount to those belts' full capacity, so players can plan by exact throughput or by "I'll just run N belts". Over-provisioning is fine; only undercapacity (groups carrying less than the export) raises the red warning chip — otherwise a green "All good" tick shows beside the actions. Between the two sits an amber nudge for structurally redundant groups: if a whole group could be deleted with the remaining groups' built belts still covering the export (judged on whole-belt capacity, e.g. 8+1+1+1 belts for a 10-belt export, or one group whose belts already carry everything), the chip says how many groups are redundant — deliberate headroom where every group is still needed is never flagged. Adding a group never rebalances fine-tuned amounts — the new group just picks up whatever the export still lacks (0 if fully allocated), with "Split evenly" available to rebalance on demand; per-mark total chips sum the whole belts to build across groups (three groups of 3× Mk.5 totals 9, not the ceiling of the fractional sum). A lone group tracks the export amount (never shrinking a deliberate over-provision on reopen). Groups persist with the plan (saved per part + destination factory on the export calculator settings, so they ride along in save/load/share; older saves initialize lazily).
- **Pipes for fluid exports**: fluid exports swap the Belts section for a Pipes section with the same group mechanics — Mk.1 (300 m³/min) and Mk.2 (600 m³/min) pipelines, pipe groups with even splitting, the All good / mismatch status and per-mark totals. Pipe groups persist separately from belt groups on the same export calculator settings. Pipeline Mk.1/Mk.2 icons pulled from the wiki into the building assets (64/256px).
- Fluid Trucks (added to the game in patch 1.2, 3,200 m³) are now supported: the Truck column calculates fluid exports as "Fluid Trucks" with the proper Fluid Truck (tanker) icon — new vehicle asset pulled from the wiki — instead of telling you to package the fluid. Drones and Tractors genuinely cannot carry raw fluids (drones/tractors have no fluid variant; packaged fluids are solids and travel as normal cargo), so for fluid exports those two columns are greyed out with a note saying to package first. The Fluid Freight Car stays at 1,600 m³.
- Tidied the Export Calculator tray: it now reads as an actual recessed tray (darker background with an inset lip, breathing room top and bottom), and the mixed thick/thin borders between the transport columns were unified into single thin dividers matching the satisfaction table's row lines — the dividing lines under the accuracy chips are gone, so each column's header visually attaches to its content. The tray also grows with its content instead of clipping at a fixed 250px height (scrolling beyond ~800px).
- Calculator polish: the Train's round-trip input icon is now a train instead of a refresh glyph; the Drone column gained a disabled "N/A" placeholder where the other vehicles have Start Timer (its rows now line up, helped by no longer reserving validation-message space under the drone time input); the belt group delete button matches the factory header's square outlined red trash button; and `tooltip-info` icons no longer sit ~3px below the surrounding text's baseline (FontAwesome's -0.125em shim, applied app-wide).

### Satisfaction — Jump to a requesting factory

- Each export chip in the satisfaction table now carries a small blue eye button on its right-hand edge that jumps straight to the requesting factory. Clicking the chip itself still selects that destination in the Export Calculator, which was the only thing a chip click could do before — reading a factory's name and having the calculator open instead of the factory was the wrong assumption often enough to be worth its own control.

### Tasks — reorder by dragging

- **Tasks can be dragged into any order**, by the grip handle on the left of each row — the same handle the sidebar's factory list uses. A task's position was fixed at the moment it was added, so the only way to reprioritise a list was to delete a task and retype it at the bottom. Completed tasks drag too.
- **Done is now a checkbox** rather than a pair of buttons, and it has moved to the left of the task, next to the handle: an empty box while the task is open, filled green with a tick once it is done, matching the green struck-through title beside it. The old control was a blue button that turned into a grey one, which read as an action to take rather than a state to see at a glance.
- Delete stays on the right and is now outlined rather than a solid red block, since it was the loudest thing in the card while being the action you least often want.

### Fixes

- **Nothing said a factory's name or a task was editable.** Both are plain text until you happen to click them, and the factory name went further and showed a pointing hand on hover — the cursor for a button, on a field you type in. Both now show a text caret and underline the text under the pointer, and stay underlined while you're editing. The underline marks the words rather than filling the field, because the name input runs to most of the width of the card header and a highlight that size drew far more attention than the edit it was offering.
- **No game image anywhere in the app had a tooltip.** Hovering an item or building icon that wasn't a wiki link told you nothing, so a row of eight icons was eight guesses. The name was being passed down, but Vuetify's image component neither renders it nor forwards it to a root element — and the component wrapping it had three root nodes, which is a shape Vue drops stray attributes from silently. Every game image now carries its display name, including the group product line in the sidebar.
- **Deleting an import, or deleting a factory that supplies another one, silently stopped half way.** The plan wasn't recalculated afterwards, so the supplying factory kept producing for an import that no longer existed; deleting a factory also skipped the re-ordering, leaving a gap in the sequence (removing the first of nine factories left the rest numbered 1–9 instead of 0–8). The calculation engine copies the plan with `structuredClone`, which refuses to copy a Vue reactive proxy, and both of those actions stored exactly such a proxy: taking `factory.inputs.filter(...)` off a reactive factory reads its rows out *through* the proxy, so assigning the filtered array back puts the proxies in as the new rows. Everything after the copy was then skipped by the error. Deleting a factory only tripped it when a dependant still had another import left over — filter an array down to nothing and there are no rows left to be proxies, which is why it went unnoticed. Every place that rewrites an array this way now unwraps it first, and the engine falls back to a slower deep copy (loudly, in the console) rather than abandoning the calculation if one is ever missed again.
- A building group no longer appears to accept more Somersloops than the building has slots for. Typing 5 into a Quantum Encoder (or a Blender, or any other 4-slot building) left the 5 sitting in the field, which reads as though the planner took it — the calculation was always correct, because the engine clamps to the slot count before it does anything with the value, but the number on screen disagreed with the number being used. Entries are now clamped as they're typed rather than on the debounce that follows, and the field is re-rendered with the clamped value, so what you see is what the plan is built from. Slot counts themselves were already right for all ten amplifiable buildings (Smelter and Constructor 1, Assembler, Foundry, Refinery and Converter 2, Manufacturer, Blender, Particle Accelerator and Quantum Encoder 4) and are now asserted per building, along with the clamp, so a wrong cap can't slip in unnoticed.
- The Somersloop field's up arrow now greys out once the building's slots are full, so the cap is visible before you hit it rather than only being explained by a warning afterwards. Vuetify disables that arrow off its `max` prop, which can't be used here — with `max` set the field stops reporting out-of-range entries at all, which is exactly what the typed-value clamp above needs to see — so the increment button is supplied through the component's own slot and disabled against the slot count instead.
- Editing a building group's Somersloops no longer spins the refresh icon on the building count as well. Every input in a group shared one debounce key, so whichever field you touched, both spinners lit up; each input now has its own key and only the field you're editing spins — the same per-field behaviour the product and power generator rows already had.
- Building group chips line up again when one of them carries a wide caption. A group's chips each sit above their own caption, and a Converter running Iron Ore (Limestone) at 51% puts "461.9 MW (184.8 MW – 739.1 MW)" under the clock — far wider than the input above it, which was left hanging 14px off centre. Chips and captions are now centred on each other, so the row reads as one line of inputs no matter what's written beneath them.
- Refreshing or deep-linking the Parts browser no longer 404s. The site is a single-page app — only `index.html` exists on the server — so every route needs a rewrite in `web/vercel.json` pointing it back at the entrypoint. That was a hand-maintained list of routes, and `/parts` was never added to it when the page shipped; navigating there from inside the app worked, so only reloads and shared links broke (and the dev server serves every path from `index.html` anyway, which is why it never showed up locally). Rather than add the missing entry, the list is gone: a single catch-all now covers every route, so adding a page is once again a zero-config operation. The catch-all deliberately excludes `/_vercel/*`, which Vercel serves itself for Web Analytics and Speed Insights — swallowing those would feed the browser HTML where it expects JavaScript and kill both, silently.
- Unknown URLs now land on a proper "Page not found" page with links back to the planner and the parts browser, instead of the bare CDN 404. This is a consequence of the catch-all above: the app, not the CDN, is now what answers for a URL that matches nothing.
- The hidden sidebar's hover tray no longer sticks open when the cursor leaves the window — flinging the cursor onto a monitor to the left (or alt-tabbing to the game) used to trip the left-edge peek zone on the way out and leave the tray showing indefinitely. A peek triggered by the cursor exiting the window is now provisional (it closes after a second unless the cursor comes back in), and the tray always collapses when the window loses focus. Peeking by hovering the left edge inside the window behaves exactly as before.
- **Every automatic correction the planner makes to your plan is now reported in one place.** The dialog that told you about micro-rounding repairs now covers everything the loader puts right, grouped by factory: broken import/export links, two factories that ended up sharing an internal ID, the same item imported twice from the same factory, an import of something that isn't made any more. Each line says what was wrong and what was done about it, in plain terms. The browser alert that used to fire for these ("check the browser console for more details") is gone — it appeared before the planner had drawn anything, said nothing useful, and sent you to the developer console. That detail is still logged for anyone debugging a shared plan.
- The debug template behind "#485: Micro-rounding repair" is now "#485 + #499: Rounding & broken chain repair", and carries a broken import/export chain alongside the drifted quantities — a copied factory inheriting the original's exports, an export that no longer matches its import, an import the supplier has no record of, an export entry pointing nowhere, and two factories sharing an internal ID. Loading it raises one dialog listing both kinds of correction, with the Refinery and its copy each showing both under a single heading.
- **Loading a plan now checks the whole import/export chain and repairs what it finds.** A plan whose figures look current is deliberately not recalculated on load — that is what makes switching tabs fast — so anything already wrong stayed wrong through every reload. The check runs on every load, fixes what it can, recalculates, and tells you what it changed.
- A plan that had never been calculated — a template, or one built in code — could have its perfectly good imports and exports torn up on its very first calculation. Two checks concluded "this factory doesn't make that" from an empty satisfaction ledger, which at that moment simply hadn't been built yet. Both now hold their tongue until there is a ledger to read.
- **Ghost exports are gone.** A factory could sit there claiming to export a part to another factory that had no matching import — or worse, quietly export the wrong amount. There wasn't one cause, there were five, and each of them is fixed:
  - **Two factories could end up with the same ID.** New factory IDs were drawn at random from 0–9,999 with nothing checking whether the number was already in use, so a 60-factory plan had roughly a one-in-six chance of a collision and a 120-factory plan closer to one-in-two. Exports are recorded against the importing factory's ID, so a collision merged two factories into one as far as the dependency system was concerned: one export request covering two different importers, the wrong factory named on the export chip, the supplier under-producing because it only saw one of the two demands, and deleting either factory taking the other's imports with it. IDs are now issued against the plan so they can't collide (and never 0, which everything else reads as "no factory selected"), and a plan loaded with a collision already in it has the clash broken and its exports rebuilt.
  - **An export could be left behind with nothing in it**, which rendered as the importing factory's name under "Exports:" with no parts against it. The cleanup only deleted an emptied entry while it happened to be looking at it.
  - **Importing the same part twice from the same factory understated the export.** Only one export request is ever raised per supplier and part, and the second import overwrote the first's amount rather than adding to it — so importing 300 and 200 Iron Ingots from the same factory told the supplier only 200 were wanted. The amounts are now totalled, duplicates already saved in a plan are merged on load, and switching an import's factory onto a combination you already have clears the item so you pick again instead of silently creating one.
  - **A corrupt import could take the whole plan down with it.** An import pointing at its own factory threw, which aborted the calculation for every factory — a blank planner over one bad row. The row is now dropped with a warning and the calculation carries on.
  - **A plan that was already wrong stayed wrong.** A plan whose figures look current is deliberately not recalculated when it loads, which is what makes switching tabs fast — but it also meant a ghost saved by an older build survived every reload. Loading now checks that the export and import sides of every link agree, and recalculates when they don't. Anyone already affected gets it cleared the next time they open the planner.
- Deleting an import row no longer deletes the other unfinished import rows with it. Rows were matched by their factory and item, and every row you haven't finished filling in looks identical (no factory, no item) — so clicking the bin on one of three blank rows removed all three. The row you clicked is now the row that goes.
- Copying a factory no longer briefly claims to export everything the original does. The copy inherited the original's exports even though the importing factories are still buying from the original; the cleanup tore them down afterwards, but only after a recalculation of every factory involved.
- Copying a factory that supplies other factories no longer throws an error during the recalculation that follows: the copy carries the original's dependency requests, and cleaning up such an orphaned request could pull the entry out from under the cleanup scan itself mid-loop.
- Fuel Generators burning Rocket Fuel no longer ask for a fraction more fuel than they need — a 240-generator plant at 240% wanted 2400.002 Rocket Fuel/min instead of 2400, forcing you to hand-override the producing factory to make the two line up. The game data is at fault: the parser worked out the MW-per-item figure by dividing a generator's power by a fuel rate it had *already* rounded to five decimal places, so Rocket Fuel's 4.1666…/min burn rate left MW-per-item at 59.999952 instead of exactly 60. The planner divides a plant's total MW by that number, so the error scaled with the plant — 1,200 generators at 240% drifted by a whole 0.01/min. MW-per-item now comes straight from the fuel's energy value (3,600 MJ ÷ 60s = 60 exactly), and fuel rates are no longer rounded before they're stored. Compacted Coal was off in the same way (10.499996 instead of 10.5) and is fixed with it.
- Alternate: Super-State Computer asked for 7.199999999999999 Computers/min instead of 7.2, and the two Uranium Fuel Rod recipes had the same wobble on Crystal Oscillators and Encased Industrial Beams. The parser was working out rates as `(60 / duration) × amount`, which loses precision on the divide; multiplying first fixes all three.
- Plans you already saved with those wrong numbers baked in now repair themselves when they load. Correcting the game data fixes everything the planner recalculates, but the quantities you *asked* for — a product's amount, a generator's fuel or power figure, an import amount — are inputs to that calculation rather than outputs, so they carried the drift forward for good. Loading a plan now sweeps those for quantities sitting a rounding hair off a whole number and snaps them back, then recalculates. The check scales with the value rather than using a flat allowance, because the drift did too: 2400.002 and 12000.01 are both caught, while a quantity you genuinely chose (12000.5, or anything more than one part per million off an integer) is left exactly as you set it. Anything on a building group whose clock you dialled in at a fractional percentage is skipped entirely — that precision is deliberate. Power generators saved before the fix also carried a stale copy of the old MW-per-item figure; that's refreshed from the current game data at the same time. When anything is repaired you get a dialog listing exactly what changed — which factory, which item, and the figure before and after — so nothing is altered behind your back.

### Under the hood

- The rule that exports and imports must agree is now written down once, in `factory-management/dependency-integrity.ts`, and enforced from both ends: the app runs it when a plan loads (which is what triggers the repair recalculation above), and the test suite asserts it after every mutation it makes. Alongside the per-case regression tests there's a seeded fuzz suite that walks the demo plan through sixty random edits — deleting imports and products, retargeting imports, adding, copying and deleting factories — and re-checks the invariants after every single one, so orderings nobody thought to write a test for are covered too. A failure names the seed that reproduces it.
- The cleanup scan that removes invalid export requests was iterating an array that the very cleanup it triggers replaces underneath it, and ran all three of its checks against a request it had just deleted (recalculating both factories again for each). It now iterates a snapshot, skips requests already gone, and stops at the first check that removes one.
- `pnpm test` no longer fails at random after every test has passed. The suite's game-data server on port 3001 was never being shut down: its teardown was wired up as `globalTeardown` in the Vitest config, which is a Jest option — Vitest ignores it silently, and takes its teardown from what `globalSetup` returns instead. The listening socket then kept the process alive, Vitest's `close()` blew its 10s budget ("something prevents Vite server from exiting"), and the run exited 0 or 1 depending on timing. The closer moved into `globalSetup`'s return value, so it actually runs; it also drops live connections first, and skips closing when the port was already occupied and no server of ours was ever started.
- Power generator quantities now opt into the same whole-number snapping that product quantities already use, so any residual hair-off-an-integer arithmetic on the power path is presented as the number it means. As with products, a building group whose clock was dialled in at a fractional percentage is deliberate precision and is never snapped. The predicate that decides this moved out of `products.ts` into `factory-management/common.ts` as `hasFractionalClock`, shared by both paths.
- A new parser test sweeps every number in the generated data file for float noise — a value sitting within a rounding hair of a short decimal without being it — so this class of bug can't reappear unnoticed. Genuine repeating rates (1.6666666666666667 Crystal Oscillators/min) are left alone; they're already the closest representable value.
- The three per-package `pnpm-lock.yaml` files were consolidated into one at the repo root (`sharedWorkspaceLockfile: true`). Renovate only ever collects the workspace-root lockfile as a changed artifact, so with the lockfiles split every `catalog:` bump shipped as a one-file diff to `pnpm-workspace.yaml` and failed all four CI checks on `ERR_PNPM_LOCKFILE_CONFIG_MISMATCH` — deterministically, for the seven catalogued packages, with no way to fix it from the hosted Renovate app ([renovate#37485](https://github.com/renovatebot/renovate/issues/37485)). One lockfile also means one resolution per dependency instead of three that can drift: 1,490 package entries across the old files collapse to 1,091, and each package's `node_modules/.pnpm` folds into a single store at the root. The API image now copies the root lockfile plus all three manifests (`--frozen-lockfile` validates every importer in the lockfile, including the ones it won't install) and narrows the install with `--filter backend`; the build workflows gained the same filter so a job stops installing the other two packages' dependencies. `onlyBuiltDependencies` was rewritten as an `allowBuilds` map at the same time — pnpm 11 removed the old key, so it had quietly been dead config.
- Upgraded Vuetify 3.12 → 4.1, which is the Material 2 → Material 3 rewrite, with the planner's appearance deliberately held still. Five of v4's breaking changes actually reach this app, and each is compensated rather than absorbed: the MD2 `text-*` typography classes are gone (`.text-h1`–`.text-h6` were already redefined in `global.scss`, but only for size and weight — the letter-spacing came from Vuetify, so it's now stated explicitly, along with `body-1`/`body-2`/`subtitle-1`/`caption`); the global `* { margin: 0; padding: 0 }` reset is gone, which would have handed browser margins back to ~330 raw headings, paragraphs and lists; `VBtn` lost its uppercase and its wider tracking; the grid swapped negative-margin gutters for `gap`, moving column content flush to the row edges; and the breakpoints shrank (md 960→840, lg 1280→1145, xl 1920→1545), which matters because the sidebar is a `d-lg-flex`. The breakpoints are pinned back in Sass rather than only via `display.thresholds`, because that option moves the JS `useDisplay()` values while the media queries behind `.d-*`, `.v-col-*` and `.v-container` are baked into the compiled CSS — so `vite-plugin-vuetify` now builds Vuetify's styles against a settings file (about 6% of build time), which brings the container max-widths back with them.
- The subtle half of that upgrade is cascade layers: v4 puts all of its own CSS in `@layer`, and unlayered rules beat layered ones no matter how specific. Font Awesome's SVG framework injects an unlayered `.svg-inline--fa { font-size: inherit }` at runtime, so every icon in the app silently shrank to its parent's font size — 18px to 14px inside a button. The fix is `font-size: revert-layer` rather than a hardcoded size, so the component rules that outrank `.v-icon--size-*` on specificity (the 28px alert icon) still win. The same reversal caught our own `.fa-bolt` margin, which used to lose to `mr-1`'s `!important` and now beat it. Worth knowing for anything added later: a rule in `global.scss` now takes precedence over Vuetify's by default, which is the opposite of how it behaved.
- Upgraded ESLint 9 → 10 across all three packages. The one blocker was the style layer: `neostandard` (still ESLint 9-only) bundles `@stylistic/eslint-plugin` v2, which calls `SourceCode` methods ESLint 10 deleted. web now depends on `@stylistic` v5 directly and hands it to neostandard's configs in place of the bundled v2, patching the three rule configs v5's schemas no longer accept (`func-call-spacing` → `function-call-spacing`, `object-property-newline`'s `allowMultiplePropertiesPerLine` → `allowAllPropertiesOnSameLine`, and the deprecated JSX aliases this project has no use for). Swapping the plugin object beats a pnpm override, which lives in `pnpm-workspace.yaml` and breaks the install anywhere pnpm doesn't read overrides from there — the Vercel build image included. The whole shim comes out once neostandard supports ESLint 10. `@eslint/js` also moved onto the workspace catalog alongside the other shared eslint packages. Rule behaviour is otherwise unchanged; the only code change is auto-fixed indentation in three TS type annotations.

## Beta v0.5 — The "Overclocked" Update

After a long hiatus: the highly anticipated Overclocking and Somersloop support via Building Groups, a huge Power update, an item-centric Parts & Recipes browser, and a planner-wide UI refresh.

### Building Groups — Overclocking & Somersloops

- Each product and power generator can now be split into Building Groups, letting you plan how production lines are physically laid out in your world. Each group has its own building count and clock speed, and the planner keeps them in sync with the production targets.
- Implemented Somersloops (production amplification) on product building groups: per-building somersloop input clamped to each building's slot count (smelter/constructor 1, assembler/foundry/refinery/converter 2, manufacturer/blender/particle accelerator/quantum encoder 4; packager and generators cannot be amplified). Output (product + byproducts) is boosted by `1 + filled/slots`, power by `(1 + filled/slots)²`, both stacking with overclocking per the wiki formulas (fully slooped at 250% ≈ 13.43× base power). Ingredient consumption is never amplified — the item's ingredient demand is discounted to what the machines actually consume, and effective/remaining buildings, rebalancing, part reverse-solving and remainder actions are all somersloop-aware. Covered by a new unit suite (`building-groups/somersloops.spec.ts`) and TDD suite (`tdd/building-groups/somersloops.spec.ts`, refs `BG-E-S-PROD`).
- The "Open/Close Building Groups" bar shows the total Somersloops (products) and Power Shards (products + power producers) the item's groups consume, always visible with 0 when unused — both resources are rare, so their usage needs to be visible at a glance. Shards follow the game rule of 1 per building per 50% clock above 100% (150% = 1, 200% = 2, 250% = 3).
- Reworked how Building Groups are opened: the toggle moved from the cramped top-right control strip to a full-width "Open/Close Building Groups" bar underneath each product and power producer (chevron up when closed, down when open), which expands the groups as a tray below it (red with a warning when the groups have a problem). - The "Open Building Groups" bar is disabled while the product has no item/recipe selected.
- Implemented `isEvenlyBalanced` logic for Product Groups to improve user feedback and action button states.
- Added comprehensive TDD tests for Building Groups: Creation, Deletion, Action Buttons, and Item Editing.

### Performance — the reactivity overhaul

- Massively reduced the reactive cost of recalculations. The calculation engine used to rebuild parts / metrics dictionaries directly on the live (reactive) plan on every pass — ~98% of those writes left values unchanged, yet each one still fired deep watchers (localStorage persistence, the sidebar factory list, and, catastrophically, Vue Devtools' sync store subscription, which deep-traverses the entire plan per write and made adding a factory hang for minutes on large plans). `calculateFactory` / `calculateFactories` now run the engine on a plain `structuredClone` of the plan and diff-commit only genuine changes back onto the live objects, preserving object identity throughout. A no-op recalculation now performs zero reactive writes (verified down from ~2,400 on the demo plan), a product edit fires ~30 watcher events instead of thousands, and `factoryUpdated` events are only emitted for factories whose data actually changed. Also fixed `setFactories` aliasing `previousInputs` to the live `inputs` array instead of snapshotting a copy.
- Added a browser-level reactivity test harness (`web/testing/browser/`) that drives the real planner (demo plan and Mael's MegaPlan) with Puppeteer, asserting DOM reactivity end-to-end and bounding the number of deep-watcher fires per interaction via a dev-only `window.__sfWatchCounter` hook.
- Added a 124-factory stress-test plan (4× Mael's MegaPlan with remapped ids, `factory-setups/stress-plan.ts`, loadable in dev via `window.__sfLoadStressPlan`) plus a browser stress harness (`stress.e2e.mjs`) that expands every factory, materializes ~294k DOM nodes, and measures edit→DOM-settle latency, DOM mutations, watcher fires and main-thread long tasks per edit. It confirms calculation churn is solved at scale (~37 fires, ~65 DOM mutations per edit; a no-op recalc of all 124 factories is 0 reactive writes / ~240ms) and pinpointed the next bottleneck: full-plan deep watchers re-traversing the whole plan per flush plus the persistence `JSON.stringify`, blocking edits ~4–6.5s on a 124-factory plan.
- Eliminated every full-plan deep watcher. Plan persistence is now event-driven instead of deep-watched: calculation commits (`factoryUpdated` / `calculationsCompleted`) trigger a debounced save that stringifies the raw tree (`toRaw` — several times faster than stringifying through proxies), explicit store mutations schedule saves directly, and direct mutations that bypass the calculator (renames, hidden toggles, tasks) are caught by a periodic compare-and-save plus a flush on `pagehide`/tab-hide — verified in the browser harness by reloading and asserting both a calculated edit and a non-calc factory rename survive. The sidebar factory list now watches an id fingerprint instead of deep-watching the plan, and the factories summary watches row count. Profiled result: Vue's deep-watch `traverse` no longer appears in edit profiles at all; edit→DOM-settle on the 124-factory stress plan dropped from ~5–6.5s to ~2.5–3.5s (measurement overhead included), with the remainder being component-update sweep and native style/layout across the ~294k-node DOM — the rendering rework's territory.
- Reworked input responsiveness around the faster engine. Numeric inputs now split "echo" from "recalculate": the typed value lands in the data instantly, and only the whole-plan recalculation is debounced — at 250ms (down from 750ms), with a consistent spinner beside every debounced input (product amounts, ingredients, byproducts, building counts, power producers, import quantities and building groups) so the pending recalc is visible. A/B-measured in-browser: raw no-debounce edits settle in ~90–180ms on the demo plan but ~580ms/keystroke on a 124-factory plan, and — decisively — without a debounce the recalc rewrites derived inputs (e.g. Requires quantities) mid-typing, corrupting entered values; the 250ms window is the guard against that (typing cadences ≤200ms/key are safe). A dev-only `window.__sfDebounceMs` override supports future A/B tests. Follow-up idea: suppress write-back to focused inputs, which would allow dropping the debounce entirely.
- Adding or removing a factory now persists and announces immediately (`factoryUpdated` + scheduled save) instead of waiting for the periodic safety net — previously a freshly added factory wasn't saved (or seen by sync) until up to 10 seconds later. The persistence safety-net interval is now 5s, and a dev-only `window.__sfLoadsCompleted` counter exposes the `loadingCompleted` event to browser tests.

### Fixes

- Quantities no longer come back a rounding-hair off what you typed (the reverse-solve rounding artifact). Typing a whole-number ingredient quantity (e.g. 1234 Crude Oil for Plastic) reverse-solves the product amount, which is often a repeating decimal (822.666…); capped at the game's 3-decimal precision, the recomputed ingredient then displayed as 1234.001 (or 1233.999 from older under-rounded data). Since exact round-trips are mathematically impossible with both sides quantised at 3dp, quantities within 0.002 of a whole number now snap to it (`snapNearInteger`) — at that precision it is vanishingly unlikely the user wanted 120.001 rather than 120. The snap is context-aware via a new `clockSetByUser` flag on building groups: when the user has dialled in a fractional overclock themselves (e.g. 223.333%), that precision is deliberate and derived quantities are kept exact — a converter at that clock still shows the game-accurate 535.999 Stone — whereas solver-derived fractional clocks (a typed quantity re-solving to 42 buildings @ 97.9365%) don't suppress the snap. It also never collapses tiny legitimate quantities (0.001 of a byproduct) to zero. Covered by unit tests on the snap math, engine round-trips and the user-clock exemption, plus a browser test typing into a real Requires input.
- Switching factory tabs no longer recalculates the whole plan. `initFactories` used to unconditionally re-run the calculation engine on every load, even though a tab's stored data is already fully calculated — on large plans this blanked the screen and hung for 4–5 seconds every time you changed tabs. Recalculation now only runs when a data migration actually backfilled a missing field (old saves); an already-current plan loads instantly. Also fixed a listener leak in `BuildingGroups.vue`, which registered a global `factoryUpdated` handler on mount but never removed it on unmount — leaked handlers accumulated across tab switches and multiplied the cost of every recalculation.
- Loading a plan template no longer zeroes out its fuel-based power generation. Producers defined only by a power amount (as plan templates are) got their default building group before any calculation had derived a building count, leaving a 0-building group — which the "building groups are sacrosanct" recalculation then faithfully synced the producer down to. A group set totalling zero buildings while its item has real ones is now treated as degenerate and rebuilt from the item, and the load-time migration no longer backfills groups for uncalculated producers. This also self-heals previously saved plans carrying such zero-groups.
- Fixed variable-power buildings (Particle Accelerator, Converter, Quantum Encoder) consuming almost no power in the planner (e.g. a Nuclear Pasta accelerator at 250% with full Somersloops showed 1.3 MW instead of ~13.4 GW). Two bugs compounded: the building-group power calculation read the flat buildings map, which only holds these buildings' 0.1 MW standby draw, instead of the recipe's real power; and the parser treated `mVariablePowerConsumptionFactor` as the maximum draw when it is actually the range *on top of* the constant (so Nuclear Pasta emitted 500–1000 MW avg 750 instead of the correct 500–1500 MW avg 1000). The parser formula is fixed (game data regenerated as v1.2-02), recipes now carry `minPower`/`maxPower`, and the planner calculates with the average while displaying the oscillation range — building group rows and product power chips show "avg (min – max)", and the factory Power & Buildings card plus the world power statistics show the peak draw alongside the average so grids can be sized against tripping. World power statistics now also sum the group-derived per-factory figures (accounting for overclocks and Somersloops) instead of the 100%-clock building requirements.
- Building groups are now sacrosanct during recalculation: the Recalculate action and plan loading never rebalance groups (counts, clocks, somersloops, matrix toggles) — when an item disagrees with its groups, the item's quantity is adjusted to match the groups instead, never the other way around. Regular item edits still rebalance groups as before (the sync feature), but an edit that doesn't change the effective building requirement now leaves a custom group split untouched. Guarded by a dedicated test suite (`building-groups/recalculate.spec.ts`).
- Editing a building group's output value no longer blows away a hand-set overclock. Previously, entering (or re-entering) an output on a group that was already overclocked would re-solve the whole group — e.g. 1 Converter @ 223.333% became 3 Converters @ 75% — because the solver preferred spreading the work across more buildings at a sub-100% clock. When an overclock is set, the group's building count is now preserved and only the clock is rescaled to hit the new output (falling back to a full re-solve only if that would exceed the game's 250% cap).
- Fixed a synced product's qty/min not matching its building groups to 3 decimal places. The item quantity was derived from the effective building count rounded to 4dp (e.g. 1 Converter @ 223.333% with a somersloop rounded to 3.35, giving 3.35 × 120 = 402), while the group produced 401.999; the quantity is now computed from the full-precision effective building count so it matches the group output exactly. A group's ingredient parts are likewise now computed straight from the recipe rate and the group's own clock rather than round-tripping through the item's amount-derived requirements, so they no longer drift by 0.001 when the item quantity changes. Covered by a Converter "solidification" spot-check (223.333% + one somersloop → 22.333 Reanimated SAM, 535.999 Limestone, 401.999 Iron Ore).
- Fixed the effective building count rounding each group to 3 decimal places when clocks support 4 (e.g. one Converter at 223.33% produced 267.96/min instead of the in-game-correct 267.996/min). Caught by a new Converter spot-check suite verified against in-game values, covering ingredients, Somersloop-amplified output and the variable power range at 250% with/without Somersloops and at 223.33%.
- Fixed editing a building group's building count updating the product's amount but not its ingredient requirements (or the factory's parts/power) until some other edit forced a recalculation. The calculation passes ran against the pre-writeback amounts and the gap was previously masked by a Vuetify labs `VNumberInput` quirk that spuriously triggered a second recalculation from the UI. `calculateFactory` now re-runs itself once when a building-group sync actually changes item amounts.
- Fixed the "Building Groups have a problem!" state falsely triggering when editing a group with sync ON (e.g. adding a Somersloop): the problem flag was computed before the group→item writeback and never refreshed, so a single calculation pass (what the UI runs) left it stuck red against stale item data. It is now recomputed after the writeback.
- Fixed group→item writeback (`checkForItemUpdate`) running on every recalculation, which replaced exact user-entered amounts with float-degraded values (e.g. 123 → 122.999…) and stomped the power producer's `updated` direction. It now only runs for edits originating from a building group.
- Fixed critical bug where `syncBuildingGroups` ignored the `buildingGroupItemSync` flag, causing unwanted rebalances.
- Fixed `deleteBuildingGroup` logic to maintain sync disabled after multiple groups are reduced to one, ensuring intentional imbalances are preserved.
- Fixed `BuildingGroups.vue` to ensure metrics are recalculated immediately on mount and on all relevant factory updates.
- Corrected default building count for newly added building groups to match current UI design (default 1).
- Updated "Evenly balance" button to work even when sync is disabled, providing better manual control.
- Fixed power producer building groups being excluded from the factory's total building requirements.
- Fixed `addPowerProducerToFactory` dropping the `ingredientAmount` option — it now seeds the fuel rate, matching the syncState mapping.
- Fixed the product building count input displaying raw float noise (e.g. 1.3333333333333333); it now displays at 3dp while keeping the underlying value precise.
- Product building count edits with negative values are now clamped to 1.
- `getPowerRecipe` no longer crashes on an unknown recipe id.
- Items from old saves without a `buildingGroups` field no longer crash the parts calculation before migration runs.
- Items that no recipe can produce (Leaves, Wood, Mycelia, alien remains, power slugs, SAM, FICSMAS Gift) are no longer selectable as products ([#390](https://github.com/satisfactory-factories/application/issues/390)). The parser now strips any part that isn't the product of a production recipe or a power-generation byproduct from `items.parts` (guarded by tests), and the game data was regenerated as v1.2-01. These items remain available as raw resources and biomass burner fuel.
- Fixed the Statistics section starting hidden for first-time visitors (`Boolean('false')` is `true` — the same string-to-boolean bug previously fixed for the Factories Summary).
- The per-factory Satisfaction section now renders its Power & Buildings card for factories that only contain fuel-less power generators (previously it required at least one part to exist, hiding the power figures entirely).
- The collapsed Statistics strip's "Generated" chip now uses the same grey generation colour as the power table and sidebar, instead of yellow.
- Unpackaged liquids that are also a Raw Ingredient (e.g. Crude Oil / Water) are no longer counted twice as both Raw Import and Production ([#431](https://github.com/satisfactory-factories/application/issues/431)), and are represented on the Satisfaction section via an "Unpackaged" badge.
- Byproducts (e.g. Water produced as a byproduct of Aluminum Scrap) are now considered "Recycled" into the system: the planner no longer incorrectly requests additional imports for them, and such items are marked with a "Recycled" badge on the Satisfaction section ([#243](https://github.com/satisfactory-factories/application/issues/243)).
- It is no longer possible to enter more than .001 of precision into an item's quantity field, as the game doesn't operate any lower than that; fixed a few rounding error bugs in the process ([#54](https://github.com/satisfactory-factories/application/issues/54)).
- Deleting a factory that imported from other factories no longer displays false "corrupted data" error messages for those factories ([#398](https://github.com/satisfactory-factories/application/issues/398)).
- Fixed several test-only issues related to stale DOM elements and floating point precision.

### Under the hood

- Migrated the repository to a pnpm workspace (per-package lockfiles) and moved to Node 24; Node 26 is also supported now that jsonwebtoken 9.0.3 dropped its transitive `SlowBuffer` dependency.
- Modernised the routing/plugin stack: vue-router 4 → 5 (the deprecated `unplugin-vue-router` was merged into vue-router core, so the separate package is gone and the Vite plugin now comes from `vue-router/vite`), the unmaintained `vite-plugin-vue-layouts` is replaced by its community successor `vite-plugin-vue-layouts-next`, and the unplugin trio jumped to their new versioning scheme (auto-import 21, vue-components 32, fonts 2 — ESM-only, config unchanged). Pinia 2 → 4 with `@pinia/testing` 2 (stores were already setup-style, so no code changes). Verified end-to-end in a real browser: routes, the `/recipes` → `/parts` redirect, layouts, and plan persistence across navigation all work with no console errors.
- Upgraded TypeScript 5.9 → 6.0 (the final JS-based line, semantically aligned with the new Go-native TS 7) and vue-tsc 2 → 3. TS 6 turns pre-TS-7 deprecations into errors, which drove some genuine cleanup: removed the deprecated `baseUrl` from tsconfigs (paths are anchored with `./` now; parsing's alias config was dead and simply deleted), moved the backend to `module`/`moduleResolution` `nodenext`, removed the long-warned-about `defineProps`/`withDefaults`/`defineEmits` compiler-macro imports from 11 components, and deleted two dead template refs vue-tsc 3 now correctly flags as unused. TS 7 itself waits until vue-tsc/typescript-eslint/ts-jest support its new API (expected after TS 7.1).
- Migrated linting to ESLint 9 flat config across all three packages. The dead `eslint-config-standard` / `eslint-plugin-node` / `eslint-config-vuetify` v1 stack is replaced by `neostandard` (standard's flat-config successor) plus `eslint-plugin-vue` 10 and `@vue/eslint-config-typescript` 14 in web, and `typescript-eslint` + `eslint-plugin-import-x` in backend/parsing. Rule behaviour is preserved (the v1 vuetify config's style rules were ported verbatim); the only code changes are auto-fixed spacing in TS type literals and two newly-caught nits (an unused catch binding and a ternary-as-statement).
- Upgraded the web build/test toolchain across two majors: Vite 5 → 7, Vitest 2 → 4 (plus @vitejs/plugin-vue 6, coverage-v8 4, jsdom 29, vite-plugin-vue-devtools 8, @types/node 24). This clears **all 37 known vulnerabilities** in the web package, including a critical arbitrary-file-read/execute hole in Vitest's UI server. Test-suite adaptations for Vitest's new mock semantics (`resetAllMocks` now restores real implementations and `spyOn` reuses existing mocks, so a few tests needed their spy history cleared explicitly).
- Upgraded every dependency across web, backend and parsing to its latest patch/minor version (highlights: Vuetify 3.7 → 3.12, Vue 3.5.40, Vue Flow 1.48, sass 1.101, Mongoose 8.24, Express 4.22, jsonwebtoken 9.0.3 — which fixes the backend crashing on Node 26, so Node 26 is now supported). Vuetify's `VNumberInput` graduated from labs along the way with two behaviour changes that needed adapting: it now rounds typed values to whole numbers by default (the planner configures unrestricted decimal precision globally, keeping fractional per-minute rates working), and it mis-handles the `v-model.number` modifier (silently dropping every keystroke), so the redundant `.number` modifiers were removed from all number inputs. (vue-router was held at 4.4.x during this pass, then upgraded to 5 in the routing-stack modernisation above.)
- `pnpm test` no longer runs Vitest in watch mode (it never finished in CI), and tests run with an in-memory `Storage` implementation under Node 24+.
